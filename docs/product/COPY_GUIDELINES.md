## 1. Purpose of This Document

This document defines the constitutional communication doctrine of the product.

Its purpose is to answer a durable question:

> How should this product speak to users?

This document governs communication across every product touchpoint, including:

- UI copy
- AI explanations
- Notifications
- Empty states
- Error messages
- Warnings
- Recommendations
- Coaching
- Reports
- Monthly summaries
- Scenario simulations
- Forecasts

This document does not define:

- Marketing campaigns
- Brand slogans
- SEO copy
- Advertising language
- Fonts
- UI layout
- Components
- Design tokens
- Localization implementation
- Content management systems
- Exact production strings

The purpose of product communication is not to persuade users into trusting the product. It is to help users understand financial reality, evaluate evidence, recognize risk, consider responsible options, and remain in control of their decisions.

## 3. Communication North Star

The product should sound calm, clear, respectful, educational, honest, and practical.

It should sound like a careful financial companion. It should not sound like a judge, salesperson, motivational speaker, debt collector, gambling interface, or AI authority.

The product should speak in a way that helps users say:

- I understand what is happening.
- I understand why it matters.
- I know what is calculated and what is assumed.
- I can see what risk exists without feeling shamed.
- I can decide what to do next.

The product should reduce confusion without hiding seriousness. It should reduce panic without weakening warnings. It should be supportive without becoming falsely reassuring.

## 4. Communication Hierarchy

Product communication should follow a constitutional order:

1. Reality
2. Evidence
3. Interpretation
4. Recommendation
5. User Decision

Communication must not begin with persuasion, recommendation, or coaching before establishing financial reality.

Reality means the user can understand the current financial state: income, obligations, debt pressure, due dates, cash flow, survival budget, risk, and relevant constraints.

Evidence means the user can see the facts, calculations, assumptions, thresholds, or source signals behind important statements.

Interpretation means the product explains what the evidence may imply, while keeping calculated truth separate from explanation.

Recommendation means the product may suggest a responsible next consideration, but only after reality and evidence are clear.

User Decision means the user remains the final decision-maker. Communication should prepare the user to decide; it should not simulate consent or replace judgment.

This hierarchy applies across AI explanations, recommendations, reports, forecasts, simulations, warnings, empty states, and errors. It protects the product from recommendation-first design and from communication that sounds confident before it has earned trust.

This hierarchy remains valid when AI generates the communication. AI may participate in wording, explanation, and synthesis, but it may not invert the order by leading with persuasion, recommendation, or confident interpretation before reality and evidence are established.

## 5. Core Communication Commitments

Each commitment in this section governs future product communication across copy, coaching, warnings, reports, summaries, scenarios, forecasts, and AI explanations.

### 5.1 Clarity Before Cleverness

**Constitutional Source**

This commitment derives from the higher constitutional requirement that financial reality must become understandable before the product becomes advisory, expressive, or optimized.

**Principle**

Copy must help users understand financial reality before it tries to sound polished.

**What this means**

Communication should use plain, direct language. A user should not need financial expertise to understand what the product is saying.

Clarity includes explaining terms, separating facts from interpretation, and naming constraints directly. Cleverness, personality, or elegance may never make the financial meaning harder to understand.

**What this rejects**

- Wordplay that obscures meaning
- Decorative copy
- Expert-gated language
- Ambiguous financial terms
- Clever tone during serious risk
- Copy that sounds polished but does not help judgment

### 5.2 Dignity Under Financial Stress

**Constitutional Source**

This commitment derives from the constitutional duty to protect dignity for users facing debt, limited cash flow, missed payments, income pressure, or uncertainty.

**Principle**

The product must never shame users for debt, risk, missed payments, low income, limited options, or financial confusion.

**What this means**

Communication should treat financial pressure as a real condition, not a character flaw. Warnings may be direct, but they must not humiliate. Reports may show difficult trends, but they must not frame the user as failing.

The product should help users face reality with enough dignity to keep going.

**What this rejects**

- Shame language
- Moral judgment
- Failure framing
- Blame
- Panic as motivation
- Copy that turns debt into identity

### 5.3 Evidence Before Advice

**Constitutional Source**

This commitment derives from the product's deterministic finance foundation, trust-through-evidence design doctrine, and roadmap capabilities such as recommendations, reports, simulations, forecasts, and AI coaching.

**Principle**

Important statements must be grounded in visible facts, calculations, assumptions, or source signals.

**What this means**

Before the product recommends, warns, forecasts, or explains, it should make the basis of the statement available. The user should be able to understand what the product is responding to.

Evidence does not need to overwhelm the user. It must be present, inspectable, and proportional to the importance of the statement.

Numerical communication is part of evidence. Amounts, dates, percentages, thresholds, and estimates should be communicated with appropriate precision. Copy must never create false certainty through unnecessary numerical detail. Rounded values should not imply exactness. Estimates must remain visibly estimates. Precision should always be proportional to evidence.

**What this rejects**

- Advice without basis
- Black-box recommendations
- Risk labels without explanation
- AI confidence without source
- Reports that summarize without showing why
- Forecasts with hidden assumptions
- Unnecessary numerical precision that creates false certainty
- Rounded values presented as exact facts

### 5.4 Agency Before Persuasion

**Constitutional Source**

This commitment derives from the constitutional requirement that users remain in control of financial decisions and that the product supports rather than replaces judgment.

**Principle**

Copy should support decisions, not pressure users into them.

**What this means**

Communication should invite consideration, clarify tradeoffs, and preserve choice. It should not manipulate urgency, guilt, fear, or optimism to push the user toward a decision.

The product may say that a situation is risky. It may not pretend that the user's decision has already been made by the system.

The product should encourage ownership of decisions while preserving dignity. Communication may explain consequences and accountability, but it must not assign moral blame.

**What this rejects**

- Persuasive pressure
- False urgency
- "You must" framing
- Copy that closes off alternatives
- Recommendations presented as commands
- AI language that sounds like authority
- Responsibility framed as blame
- Accountability framed as moral failure

### 5.5 Calm Without Concealment

**Constitutional Source**

This commitment derives from the design doctrine of restraint as protection and from the constitutional requirement to communicate risk without panic or evasion.

**Principle**

Communication should reduce panic without hiding meaningful risk, urgency, or consequences.

**What this means**

The product should speak calmly because financial stress already narrows attention. Calmness must not become under-warning. If a payment deadline, negative cash flow, or survival budget pressure matters, copy must make that clear.

The product should avoid both alarmism and false comfort.

Urgency should never be manufactured. Communication should distinguish informational, optional, important, high-priority, and high-risk situations by explaining why a message belongs at that level. Calm communication must not weaken necessary warnings.

**What this rejects**

- Panic language
- Softening serious risk into vague reassurance
- Hiding urgency to preserve calm
- Dramatic warnings without explanation
- Friendly tone that masks consequences
- Under-warning in the name of restraint
- Manufactured urgency
- Severity labels without reasons

### 5.6 Uncertainty Without Vagueness

**Constitutional Source**

This commitment derives from the constitutional requirement to make uncertainty visible in forecasts, simulations, AI explanations, and future-facing outputs.

**Principle**

Forecasts, simulations, and AI explanations must communicate uncertainty clearly without becoming evasive.

**What this means**

The product should name what is known, what is assumed, and what may change. It should avoid pretending that projections are guarantees.

Uncertainty should help users reason. It should not become vague copy that avoids responsibility or leaves the user with no usable conclusion.

Estimates, projected dates, percentages, and forecasted values should remain visibly conditional. Precision should increase only when the supporting evidence justifies it.

**What this rejects**

- False precision
- Guaranteed outcomes
- Vague caveats that do not explain anything
- Confident predictions from unstable assumptions
- Forecasts that hide dependency on future behavior
- AI-generated certainty
- Estimates that read like exact outcomes

### 5.7 Education Without Superiority

**Constitutional Source**

This commitment derives from the product's educational role, inclusive design doctrine, and responsibility to support users across different levels of financial literacy.

**Principle**

The product should teach without sounding condescending, expert-gated, or morally superior.

**What this means**

Educational communication should make financial concepts more approachable. It should explain terms and tradeoffs without implying that the user should already know them.

The product should sound useful, not impressed with itself.

**What this rejects**

- Condescension
- Expert superiority
- Jargon as authority
- Explaining basic terms with impatience
- Treating financial literacy gaps as user failure
- Over-teaching when a direct explanation is enough

### 5.8 Privacy Through Plain Disclosure

**Constitutional Source**

This commitment derives from the local-first privacy design doctrine, consent-as-continuous principle, and the sensitivity of salary, debt, expenses, credit limits, due dates, household pressure, and notes.

**Principle**

Users should understand data sensitivity, consent, and data movement in plain language.

**What this means**

Privacy communication should not rely on fine print or legalistic opacity. If financial information moves between systems, is summarized for AI, or is processed outside a local boundary, the communication should make that meaningful movement understandable.

Consent should be continuous where the context changes. The user should not be surprised by how sensitive financial information is used.

**What this rejects**

- Fine-print privacy
- One-time consent that hides future data movement
- Vague data-use language
- Overly technical disclosure
- Normalizing casual sharing of financial details
- Privacy that is technically correct but emotionally unclear

### 5.9 Turkish Financial Reality Without Stereotype

**Constitutional Source**

This commitment derives from the product's Turkish-first UX requirement, the design doctrine of local financial context, and the strategy's focus on serving Turkish users' real financial conditions.

**Principle**

Copy should reflect Turkish personal finance realities without reducing users to cultural assumptions.

**What this means**

Communication should account for recurring salary cycles, credit card behavior, installment culture, inflation pressure, changing household obligations, and short-term liquidity pressure when those realities matter.

This is not localization mechanics. It is product understanding expressed through language.

**What this rejects**

- Translationese
- Imported fintech jargon
- Cultural stereotypes
- Generic global advice that ignores local reality
- Mixed-language UI copy without reason
- Treating Turkish-first communication as translation afterthought

### 5.10 Recovery-Oriented Communication

**Constitutional Source**

This commitment derives from the design doctrine of recoverability and the constitutional requirement to help users recover from mistakes, changed assumptions, stale information, and failed system states.

**Principle**

Errors, corrections, and changed assumptions should help users recover, not feel punished.

**What this means**

When something is missing, invalid, failed, stale, or changed, copy should explain what happened, what it means, and what can be done next. It should not blame the user or make the product feel unsafe to explore.

Recovery includes emotional recovery. The user should feel able to correct, retry, revise, or continue.

**What this rejects**

- Punitive errors
- Dead-end messages
- Blaming the user
- Technical failure language without a next step
- Treating changed assumptions as user failure
- Copy that makes exploration feel risky

## 6. Voice Doctrine

The product voice should be:

- Calm
- Plain
- Respectful
- Direct
- Educational
- Evidence-based
- Nonjudgmental
- Human but not overly intimate
- Serious but not cold

The product should sound steady in difficult moments. It should not perform cheerfulness around debt pressure, negative cash flow, or high-risk situations.

The product may be supportive. It must not become sentimental, motivational, or emotionally intrusive. Sensitive financial data should not be surrounded by exaggerated warmth or forced familiarity.

The product voice belongs to the product itself, not to any AI provider or generated response. AI is one communication mechanism within the product; it must not become the dominant product voice.

The voice rejects:

- Motivational cliches
- Alarmist language
- Sales language
- Shame
- False reassurance
- AI mystique
- AI voice drift
- Overfriendly intimacy around sensitive data
- Licensed financial-advisor tone
- Humor in serious financial risk contexts

## 7. Tone by User State

The product's voice remains stable, but tone may adapt to the user's state.

Tone adaptation must never change constitutional commitments. The product may become more direct in risk, more explanatory in onboarding, or more concise in summaries, but it must remain clear, respectful, evidence-based, and nonjudgmental.

For first-time orientation, communication should reduce uncertainty and explain what the product can and cannot do.

For stable monthly review, communication should help the user understand the current month without creating artificial urgency.

For high-risk financial pressure, communication should be direct, specific, and calm. It should not intensify fear.

For priority and severity, communication should make the level understandable. Informational, optional, important, high-priority, and high-risk messages should differ because the underlying consequence differs, not because the product wants more attention.

For negative cash flow, communication should name the constraint clearly and avoid optimization language before survival pressure is understood.

For debt prioritization, communication should distinguish required payments from optional extra payoff choices.

For late or near-due payment risk, communication should make timing and consequence clear without shame.

For scenario exploration, communication should make hypothetical status visible and prevent confusion with current reality.

For AI explanation, communication should remain educational, bounded, and downstream from deterministic outputs.

For error or failed provider states, communication should explain what happened, preserve trust, and offer a recovery path.

For monthly summaries or reports, communication should support continuity, show change over time, and avoid scorecard shame.

## 8. Reusable Explanation Model

Every meaningful explanation should help users understand:

1. What happened?
2. Why?
3. What does it mean?
4. What assumptions exist?
5. What should the user consider next?

This model applies across:

- AI explanations
- Recommendations
- Reports
- Forecasts
- Simulations
- Risk communication
- Monthly summaries
- Error and failure states

The model does not require every explanation to become long. The level of detail should match the importance and risk of the situation. However, the communication should not skip the reasoning layer when an output could affect a financial decision.

"What happened?" establishes reality.

"Why?" connects the output to evidence, rules, thresholds, calculations, inputs, or changed assumptions.

"What does it mean?" provides interpretation while keeping interpretation separate from calculation.

"What assumptions exist?" makes uncertainty, stale data, scenario conditions, or provider limits visible.

"What should the user consider next?" preserves agency by offering a responsible next consideration rather than a command.

This model reinforces the communication hierarchy: reality before evidence, evidence before interpretation, interpretation before recommendation, and recommendation before user decision.

The model also governs numerical explanation. When a number matters, communication should help users understand what the number represents, why it changed or appeared, whether it is exact or estimated, what assumptions shape it, and how much confidence the evidence supports.

## 9. Financial Risk Communication

Risk communication must be clear, specific, proportionate, and respectful.

Risk and urgency language should distinguish:

- Informational
- Optional
- Important
- High Priority
- High Risk

These levels are communication meanings, not visual or implementation categories. Communication should explain why something belongs to a given level rather than relying only on a label.

Risk copy should explain why the risk exists. It should distinguish:

- The fact or calculation
- The risk interpretation
- The consequence or tradeoff
- The possible next consideration

Risk labels shown to users must remain limited to:

- `Düşük`
- `Orta`
- `Yüksek`

Critical financial situations should appear as reasons under `Yüksek Risk`, not as a separate visible risk level.

Risk communication should not soften serious situations into vague reassurance. It should also avoid panic language that narrows the user's ability to think.

High priority should reflect a meaningful need for attention. High risk should reflect a meaningful financial consequence. Neither should be used to manufacture urgency.

Risk communication rejects:

- Moral judgment
- Hidden severity
- Vague warnings
- Risk labels without explanation
- Panic language
- Scorecard shame
- Treating high risk as personal failure
- Manufactured urgency
- Calm wording that weakens necessary warnings

## 10. Recommendation and Coaching Language

Recommendations are suggestions, not commands.

Coaching language should help the user understand a responsible next consideration. It should not imply that the product has made the user's financial decision.

Recommendation and coaching copy must respect this order:

1. Mandatory living expenses
2. Minimum required debt payments
3. Survival budget protection
4. Risk reduction
5. Optional optimization

The product should not recommend extra debt payoff if the survival budget threshold cannot be protected.

Recommendations should explain their reasoning. If a recommendation is based on avalanche priority, due-date risk, interest pressure, cash-flow constraint, or forecast assumption, the communication should make that basis understandable.

Coaching language should stay educational-only. It must avoid licensed financial-advisor framing.

The product must never imply that it is providing legal advice, tax advice, regulated investment advice, or licensed financial advice. Recommendations should remain educational, evidence-grounded, and user-directed. The final decision belongs to the user.

Communication should distinguish responsibility, accountability, and blame. Responsibility means the user remains the decision-maker. Accountability means consequences should be understandable. Blame means moral judgment; the product must reject it.

Recommendation and coaching language rejects:

- "You must" framing
- "Guaranteed" outcomes
- "Best choice for you" certainty
- Overconfident payoff advice
- AI as decision-maker
- Optimization before survival protection
- Advice detached from deterministic outputs
- Legal, tax, regulated investment, or licensed financial-advice framing
- Responsibility language that becomes blame

## 11. AI Communication Doctrine

AI communication should explain deterministic outputs. It should not become the source of financial truth.

AI may help users understand risk, tradeoffs, assumptions, scenarios, forecasts, and possible next questions. It must remain bounded by deterministic calculations and minimized financial summaries.

The product's communication identity belongs to the product itself, not to AI. AI is one communication mechanism within the product. It must never become the dominant product voice.

The constitutional communication hierarchy continues to govern AI-generated explanations:

1. Reality
2. Evidence
3. Interpretation
4. Recommendation
5. User Decision

AI-generated communication must not begin with persuasion, advice, or confident recommendations before establishing reality and evidence.

AI communication should:

- Be Turkish-first
- Be educational
- Name uncertainty
- Surface assumptions
- Ask useful review questions
- Avoid unnecessary sensitive detail
- Remain useful when the provider fails
- Preserve the user's final decision authority

AI fallback copy should be calm and useful. A missing API key, timeout, validation failure, or provider error should not make the financial product feel broken.

AI communication must not imply legal, tax, regulated investment, or licensed financial advice. It should remain educational, evidence-grounded, and user-directed.

AI communication rejects:

- AI oracle framing
- Personalized certainty
- Hidden model assumptions
- Emotional over-intimacy
- Advice detached from deterministic summaries
- Licensed-advisor tone
- AI language that implies the model calculated source-of-truth values
- AI becoming the dominant product voice
- AI-generated recommendations before reality and evidence
- Legal, tax, regulated investment, or licensed financial-advice framing

## 12. Error, Empty, and Failure State Communication

Error, empty, and failure states should help users recover.

They should explain:

- What happened
- Why it may have happened, when useful and safe
- What the user can do next
- Whether the product can continue with fallback, stale, or partial information

These states must avoid blame. They must not expose secrets, raw sensitive financial data, stack traces, internal provider details, or implementation internals.

Empty states should orient the user without making missing data feel like failure. They should clarify what is needed and why it matters.

Failure states involving AI or external providers should preserve trust in the deterministic product. The product should remain useful even when optional explanatory services are unavailable.

Error, empty, and failure communication rejects:

- Punitive errors
- Technical stack traces
- Blaming the user
- Dead-end messages
- Secret exposure
- Raw financial data exposure
- Making optional provider failure feel like product failure

## 13. Forecast, Simulation, and Scenario Copy

Forecasts, simulations, and scenarios are planning tools, not promises.

Communication must distinguish current reality from hypothetical exploration. Users should understand when they are looking at actual current data, projected future outcomes, or scenario-based alternatives.

Forecast and scenario copy should make assumptions visible, including where relevant:

- Income stability
- Expense stability
- Interest rates
- Payment timing
- Minimum payment behavior
- Extra payment assumptions
- Scenario-specific changes

When a scenario changes an outcome, communication should help users understand what changed and why.

Forecast values, payoff dates, percentages, and projected balances should use precision proportional to evidence. If an output is estimated, conditional, rounded, stale, or scenario-based, communication should preserve that status rather than making the number sound exact.

Forecast, simulation, and scenario copy rejects:

- False precision
- Guaranteed payoff dates
- Hidden assumptions
- Confusing simulated and actual data
- Optimism that hides constraints
- Presenting projections as certainty
- Rounded projections that imply exactness
- Precise-looking estimates without evidence

## 14. Reports and Monthly Summary Communication

Reports and monthly summaries should help users understand change over time.

They should distinguish:

- Snapshot
- Trend
- Explanation
- Recommendation
- Open decision

Reports should orient rather than overwhelm. They should help users see what changed, what stayed stable, what risk increased or decreased, and which assumptions shaped the result.

A difficult month should not be framed as personal failure. A better month should not be over-celebrated in a way that hides ongoing risk.

Monthly communication should preserve continuity without creating dependence. Users should become better able to understand their financial situation over time, not less able to reason without the product.

Reports should communicate responsibility without blame. They may show consequences, patterns, and open decisions, but they must not turn financial outcomes into moral judgment.

Reports and monthly summaries reject:

- Scorecard shame
- Progress theater
- Contextless comparisons
- Treating each month as isolated
- Over-celebrating behavior
- Over-punishing behavior
- Trend claims without evidence
- Numerical precision that overstates certainty
- Responsibility framed as personal failure

## 15. Consent, Privacy, and Data Movement Language

Privacy communication should be plain, continuous, and emotionally understandable.

Users should understand when financial information:

- Stays local
- Is summarized
- Supports AI explanation
- May move between systems
- Is unavailable, stale, or partial

Consent should be understandable. Consent should also be continuous where the context changes. A one-time statement should not hide future meaningful data movement.

Data movement language should be proportionate. Not every technical operation needs user-facing explanation, but meaningful movement of sensitive financial information should never be implicit.

Privacy and consent copy should make the product feel careful with the user's financial life.

Consent, privacy, and data movement language rejects:

- Fine-print privacy
- One-time consent that hides future data movement
- Vague "we may use your data" language
- Overly technical disclosure
- Surprise external processing
- Treating sensitive financial data as casual content

## 16. Turkish-First Communication Doctrine

Turkish-first communication is a product doctrine, not a localization rule.

The product should be conceived in natural Turkish for Turkish users. It should not feel like English-first fintech language translated afterward.

Communication should use plain Turkish, explain financial terms when needed, and reflect Turkish personal finance realities without stereotyping users.

Relevant realities may include:

- Recurring salary cycles
- Credit card usage patterns
- Installment obligations
- Inflation pressure
- Household responsibility
- Short-term liquidity pressure
- Changing required expenses

The product should name these realities when they help understanding. It should not rely on cultural shorthand, jokes, assumptions, or stereotypes.

Risk levels shown to users must be only:

- `Düşük`
- `Orta`
- `Yüksek`

Turkish-first communication rejects:

- Translationese
- Imported fintech jargon
- Culturally generic advice
- Stereotyped household assumptions
- Mixed-language UI copy without reason
- Treating Turkish as a presentation layer rather than product understanding

## 18. Copy Review Questions

Future product communication should be reviewed against these questions before it is accepted:

- Is this clear?
- Is this respectful?
- Does this preserve user agency?
- Does this establish reality before recommendation?
- Is the evidence visible or available?
- Does the copy distinguish fact, calculation, assumption, interpretation, suggestion, and decision?
- Are amounts, dates, percentages, thresholds, and estimates communicated with precision proportional to evidence?
- Are rounded values and estimates prevented from sounding exact?
- Is risk clear without panic or shame?
- Does priority or severity language explain why the message is informational, optional, important, high priority, or high risk?
- Is urgency real rather than manufactured?
- Is uncertainty honest without becoming evasive?
- Does AI remain framed as explanation, not authority?
- Does AI follow reality, evidence, interpretation, recommendation, and user decision in that order?
- Does the product voice remain the product's voice rather than becoming the AI's voice?
- Is privacy emotionally understandable?
- Is consent understandable and continuous where financial information may move between systems?
- Is Turkish natural, plain, and context-aware?
- Does this reflect Turkish financial reality where relevant without stereotyping users?
- Can the user recover from this state?
- Does the copy avoid false reassurance?
- Does the copy avoid licensed financial-advisor language?
- Does the copy avoid legal, tax, or regulated investment-advice framing?
- Does the copy encourage responsibility without blame?
- Does the copy avoid marketing pressure?
- Does the explanation answer what happened, why, what it means, what assumptions exist, and what the user should consider next?

If communication cannot answer these questions well, it should be revised before implementation.
